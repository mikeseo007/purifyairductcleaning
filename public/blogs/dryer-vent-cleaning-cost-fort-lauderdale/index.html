Cost Factors That Change Your Price

These are the main variables that push a Fort Lauderdale dryer vent cleaning bill up or down.

Vent length and number of bends

Longer runs and multiple elbows trap lint and require more time and tools; a 30 foot, winding run typically doubles labor compared with a 10 foot straight run.

Accessibility and required access points

If a technician must enter an attic, crawlspace, or remove dryer or wall panels, labor increases. Exterior-only jobs are cheapest.

Degree of lint compaction and blockage

Loose lint cleans quickly; compacted lint, hardened debris, or partial collapse of liner needs more effort and higher cost.

Repairs and component replacement

Damaged vent collars, crushed flex duct, or corroded exterior hoods are common add-ons and usually billed separately.

Contamination from mold or pests

If mold, insects, or rodent nesting is found, expect remediation or sanitizing steps to raise cost and extend job time.

Commercial or multi-unit complexity

Multiple dryers on shared duct systems require coordinated access and often a different pricing model from single residential units.

When Should You Clean a Dryer Vent Now: Warning Signs and Safety Risks

Watch for this: A heavily clogged dryer vent is a fire risk and can damage the dryer. Clean the vent immediately if you notice any of these signs: longer than normal drying cycles, the dryer or vent feels hot to the touch, a persistent burning or musty smell when the dryer runs, visible lint build-up at the exterior vent cover, or excessive vibration in the dryer. In multi-unit buildings, one blocked branch can affect multiple units and require coordinated access. If you have had any near-fire incidents, sparks, or unusual smoke, do not run the dryer and schedule a professional inspection right away.

  • ✓Move fragile items and secure pets out of the workspace

How the Cleaning Works and Typical On-Site Timeline

A typical professional cleaning follows a predictable sequence. First, the technician does a visual inspection and notes access points, run length, and visible damage. Next, the dryer is disconnected and the dryer-to-duct connection is opened. Then the technician uses brushes, compressed air, and vacuuming tools to dislodge lint from the entire run and the exterior hood. If available, a camera inspection is used to confirm complete removal and find hidden issues. After cleaning, the technician reconnects the dryer, tests airflow, and walks you through any recommended repairs. For most residential jobs the on-site time is 30 to 90 minutes; longer, more complex jobs take 2 to 3 hours. Ask for photos or a short report so you can see the before and after condition.

Transparent Add-ons, Repairs, and Next Steps to Book

Common add-ons include vent hood replacement, flex or rigid duct replacement, bird or pest screens, sanitizing for mold remediation, and coordination for multi-unit access. Typical ballpark ranges: hood or short section replacement often runs from $50 to $200, full duct replacement is substantially higher depending on material and length, and mold remediation or sanitizing adds to cost based on contamination. To avoid surprises, request a written line-item estimate after the inspection. Local insight

Fort Lauderdale Local Insight: Coastal conditions matter

Fort Lauderdale homes often have higher indoor humidity and salt in the air compared with inland areas. Humidity encourages lint to clump and stick to duct walls, while salt can accelerate corrosion of metal vent components. Many local properties use attic runs or roof exits, which increases the chance of moisture-related buildup and occasional damage to exterior hoods. Frequently asked questions

How much do I charge to clean a dryer vent?

If you mean typical professional charges for dryer vent cleaning, expect about $80 to $120 for short, easy residential runs; $150 to $250 for average single-family jobs; and $300 or more for long or obstructed runs or commercial systems. For an accurate quote at your Fort Lauderdale property, request an on-site estimate.

How often do dryer vents need to be cleaned?

Most households should inspect or clean dryer vents every 12 months. In Fort Lauderdale, higher humidity may require more frequent attention; if you dry large loads often or notice longer cycles, clean or inspect every 6 to 9 months.

Are dryer vent cleaning services worth it?

Yes when the alternative is longer drying times, higher energy use, or increased fire risk. Professional services remove deep lint, identify damage, and address contamination that DIY cleaning can miss, making the service worthwhile for long runs, attic venting, or recurring performance issues.

How long does it take a professional to clean a dryer vent?

Most residential jobs take 30 to 90 minutes on-site. Short, exterior-only runs are on the lower end. Long runs, attic access, heavy blockages, or repairs extend the time to 2 to 3 hours or more.
